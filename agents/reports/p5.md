## The brief's P4 premise was wrong, and you re-scoped it

The brief said to reuse `resolveInternalSecret` from `_shared/secret.ts` and a
P4 wrapper `public.aigundem_internal_get_setting`. **Neither existed** — I wrote
P4 (commit `536bcfa`) and it authenticates against
`Deno.env.get('AUTOMATIONS_SECRET')`. I raised it before building on a
non-existent module; you moved the whole secret mechanism into P5 and widened
my paths. So this task also ships:

- `aigundem.internal_get_setting(p_name)` + its shim, reading
  `vault.decrypted_secrets`, **allow-listed to the single name
  `aigundem_automations_secret`**. The allow-list is the load-bearing part:
  without it this is a read-any-secret primitive, and one future mistake about
  who may EXECUTE it would expose every secret the project holds rather than
  this one.
- `_shared/secret.ts` — `resolveInternalSecret(env, rpc)`: environment first
  (so it costs nothing once an Edge secret can finally be set), Vault second
  (the only source that exists today).
- The same resolution wired into `sync-feeds` and `process-enrichments`, so all
  three internal functions now agree on where the secret comes from.

**Two consequences you should know about, both deliberate:**

1. **`process-enrichments`'s no-key path now costs one Vault read.** P4's
   version returned `skipped:"no_api_key"` with zero database calls. You cannot
   authenticate against a secret that lives in the database without reading it,
   so the tick now costs exactly one RPC. It still leases nothing, writes
   nothing and calls Claude nothing — the property that mattered survives.
2. **The auth call moved after client construction** in both functions, because
   resolution needs a client. In `sync-feeds` that also moved body parsing to
   after auth, which is the better order anyway. Slightly more than the "single
   call site" the brief described; it is the minimum that works.

## The ranking algorithm

Candidates are articles that are **in the window**, **from an active source**,
and **carry a summary whose `content_hash` still matches the article** — a stale
summary simply fails to join, so a rewritten article is ineligible until it is
re-enriched. No invalidation step exists anywhere, by construction.

They are ordered by **(per-source recency rank, `published_at` DESC, `id` DESC)**
and the top five are taken.

The effect is round-robin by source: every source's newest article first
(newest of those first), then every source's second-newest, and so on.

- Five sources published → five different sources, newest first.
- Only one source published → it still fills all five slots. That is "max one
  per source **first**, then fill", not "at most one per source".
- Six sources published → the five newest firsts.

The order is **total**, which is what makes it deterministic: `published_at` ties
— arXiv stamps a whole batch with the same minute — so the article id breaks
them, and ids are unique. Same data, same five items, same positions, every run.
Tested for determinism across repeated runs, reversed input, and shuffled input.

`blurb_tr` is the **first bullet of the stored summary**, trimmed and capped at
600 characters. **No Claude call is made anywhere in the digest path**, which is
what makes a digest buildable with no Anthropic key in existence.

**All five or none.** Items are written only once the window holds five
candidates, so `digest_items` is never partially populated and a `preparing`
digest holds zero rows. Fewer than five leaves the digest `preparing` and
reports `missing_enrichments` (addendum §E).

### The ranker lives twice — read this before trusting the tests

The ranker that **runs** is the SQL in migration 0008. It has to be: selection
and insertion must share one snapshot and one transaction, or two finalize
retries could rank against different data.

`rankDigestCandidates` in `_shared/digest.ts` is the same algorithm in
TypeScript, and it is what the 33 Jest assertions exercise. It is a
**specification and a mirror, not the production code path**.
`sql-lint-p5.test.ts` pins the SQL's exact `row_number() over (…)` clauses
against it so the two cannot drift silently, but **genuine equivalence is
unproven without a database** and is listed under NOT VERIFIED.

## The window

`window_end` = **05:00 Istanbul on the digest's own date**; `window_start` =
24 hours earlier. For `2026-08-21`: `[2026-08-20T02:00Z, 2026-08-21T02:00Z)`.

Two decisions worth stating:

- **Derived from the date, never from `now()`** (apart from defaulting the date
  itself). A retry at 03:50 UTC, a manual call and a backfill for last Tuesday
  all compute the same window. That is what makes finalize idempotent.
- **05:00 rather than midnight.** The audience is Turkish; the sources are
  mostly American. OpenAI, Anthropic and DeepMind publish during US business
  hours, which is Istanbul evening and night. A midnight boundary would push a
  story posted at 01:00 Istanbul into the *following* day's digest — a full day
  late. 05:00 is also 45 minutes before the prepare job fires, so the window is
  always closed when it is read.

Istanbul has been permanently UTC+3 since 2016, so there is no DST seam to
model. The tests assert every window is exactly 24 hours across the old EU
transition dates, and pin the offset constant — the single thing that breaks if
Türkiye ever reinstates seasonal clocks. **Postgres owns the runtime
arithmetic** (`at time zone 'Europe/Istanbul'`), so it would adapt on its own;
the TypeScript constant is the part that would need editing.

## The four cron rows

All schedules are UTC. Every job is **created with `active := false`**.

| jobname | schedule | UTC → Istanbul | calls | body |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ai-gundem-ingest` | `*/15 * * * *` | — | `/sync-feeds` | `{"max_sources": 10}` |
| `ai-gundem-ai-worker` | `*/2 * * * *` | — | `/process-enrichments` | `{"max_jobs": 3}` |
| `ai-gundem-digest-prepare` | `45 2 * * *` | 05:45 | `/build-digest` | `{"phase": "prepare"}` |
| `ai-gundem-digest-finalize` | `30,40,50 3 * * *` | 06:30/40/50 | `/build-digest` | `{"phase": "finalize"}` |

- The last finalize retry lands at 06:50 Istanbul — ten minutes of margin before
  the earliest 07:00 notification (arch-001 §2). A test computes this rather
  than trusting the comment.
- **The `X-Internal-Secret` header is read from Vault inside each job body**, so
  it is fetched when the job runs and never stored in a job definition. Rotating
  the secret needs no rescheduling. A lint assertion checks there is no literal
  that looks like a secret value.
- The URL is
  `coalesce(current_setting('app.settings.functions_url', true), 'https://eglxzbsrewbleqlstefd.supabase.co/functions/v1')`,
  so a branch or a restored project can override it without editing an applied
  migration.
- **Idempotent**: each job is unscheduled if it already exists, then
  rescheduled, then disabled.

Created disabled on purpose: an enabled `ai-gundem-ai-worker` on a project whose
Claude path has never run would march the entire backlog through its retry
budget unattended, overnight, with nobody watching.

## What is NOT verified

1. **That any of this SQL parses.** No parser was installable offline and no
   database was reachable. Syntax is inspection plus a structural scan.
2. **That the SQL ranking equals the TypeScript ranking.** The lint pins the
   clauses; only a database proves they agree. **This is the single most
   valuable thing your smoke can check** — build a digest and compare the five
   ids against `rankDigestCandidates` on the same rows.
3. **`pg_cron` and `pg_net`.** Whether `create extension` succeeds on this
   project, whether pg_net exposes `net.http_post` with the
   `timeout_milliseconds` argument, and whether the migration role may call
   `cron.schedule`/`cron.alter_job` are all unconfirmed. arch-001 §7 risk 9
   flagged exactly this.
4. **That the cron jobs can read Vault.** Jobs run as their owner; whether that
   role can select `vault.decrypted_secrets` is unverified, as is whether
   `aigundem_automations_secret` is actually present under that name.
5. **The `app.settings.functions_url` setting.** Never set, so every job will
   take the literal fallback. That is intended, but the `current_setting` branch
   has never been exercised.
6. **The digest end to end** — that `internal_digest_prepare` creates exactly
   one row per Istanbul day, that finalize is genuinely a no-op once `ready`,
   that P2's `digests_ready_is_complete` CHECK accepts what finalize writes, and
   that `position` fits `smallint` as cast.
7. **Anything Deno** — `Deno.serve`, `Deno.env`, and the `build-digest` glue.
8. **That five enriched articles will ever exist.** With no Anthropic key
   nothing is enriched, so **today the digest will correctly stay `preparing`
   forever**. That is the designed behaviour, not a failure — but it means the
   `ready` path cannot be smoke-tested until a key exists, unless you insert
   summary rows by hand.

**Do not drop `internal_get_setting` alone.** `sync-feeds` and

deployed makes every internal call fail closed as `internal_error`. Either roll
those two functions back to their P4 revision first, or set `AUTOMATIONS_SECRET`
in their environment — the resolver prefers it and never reaches the RPC.

The extensions are deliberately left alone: `pg_cron` and `pg_net` may be in use
by the unrelated application in `public`, and dropping either is not this
migration's business. Digest rows and items are also left alone — they are data,
and `digest_items` cascades from `digests` if you ever do remove one.
